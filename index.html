Most AI mistakes don't happen at the start of a conversation.

They show up later, after you've changed your mind a few times and the AI loses track of something you told it earlier. Everything it does after that is built on the mistake.

In a study of more than 200,000 simulated conversations, leading LLMs did 39% worse on average when a task was spread over several turns instead of asked all at once. (Laban et al., LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation, ICLR 2026)

Aman Sreejesh AP Research, 2026–27 See how I'm studying it

    The question

    When an LLM makes a mistake partway through a conversation, how long do you have to fix it?

    My research question, word for word

    To what extent do a language model's own prior outputs, the fragmentation of instructions across turns, and user pressure each contribute to accuracy loss in multi-turn conversation, and at what point does that loss become irreversible?

    In plain terms

    LLMs usually start a conversation well. The trouble comes later, once the instructions are spread across many messages and the model is building on its own earlier replies.

    I'm measuring how many turns pass between a model's first wrong answer and the point where even handing it the missing information no longer fixes the answer. I call that stretch the recovery window.

    Why it matters

    Spreading a task across messages makes LLMs worse.

    Average performance, relative to asking all at once

    Asked all at once 100
    Spread over several turns 61

    Laban et al., LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation (ICLR 2026), averaged across six generation tasks.

    People hold longer and longer conversations with LLMs, and many trust the answers without checking. If a model loses track of details as a conversation goes on, the longer the task, the more likely the final answer is wrong, and the person may never notice.

    What I'm testing

    Three explanations for why a conversation goes wrong.

    Earlier studies point at three different causes. I'm testing each one to see how much it shortens the recovery window. The chats below are made-up examples of each.

    1. 01

      The model's own earlier answers

      Models tend to treat what they already said as settled, even when it was wrong. A mistake from one turn gets reused in the next.

    2. 02

      Instructions given in pieces

      Real requests arrive across several messages, and details from early in the conversation get lost by the time the model answers.

    3. 03

      Pushback from the user

      When someone challenges an answer, a model sometimes drops a correct answer just because the person sounded sure.
