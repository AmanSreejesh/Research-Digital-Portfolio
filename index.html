Most AI mistakes don't happen at the start of a conversation.

They show up later, after you've changed your mind a few times and the AI loses track of something you told it earlier. Everything it does after that is built on the mistake.

In a study of more than 200,000 simulated conversations, leading AI models did 39% worse on average when a task was spread over several turns instead of asked all at once. (Laban et al., LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation, ICLR 2026)

Aman Sreejesh AP Research, 2026–27 See how I'm studying it

    When an AI makes a mistake partway through a conversation, how long do you have to fix it?

    The research question

    To what extent do a language model's own prior outputs, the fragmentation of instructions across turns, and user pressure each contribute to accuracy loss in multi-turn conversation, and at what point does that loss become irreversible?

    Chatbots usually start a conversation well. The trouble comes later, once the instructions are spread across many messages and the model is building on its own earlier replies. I'm measuring how many turns pass between a model's first wrong answer and the point where even handing it the missing information no longer fixes the answer. I call that stretch the recovery window.

    What I'm testing

    1. 01

      The AI's own earlier answers

      Models tend to treat what they already said as settled, even when it was wrong. I'm testing whether letting a model see its earlier replies changes how long a mistake can still be fixed.

    2. 02

      Instructions given in pieces

      Real requests arrive across several messages, and details from early in the conversation get lost. I split each problem's instructions in different ways to see which kinds of splitting make recovery harder.

    3. 03

      Pushback from the user

      When someone challenges an answer, a model sometimes abandons a correct answer or digs in on a wrong one. In some conversations a simulated user pushes back, so I can measure what that does to the recovery window.
